## Communities 

Roughly 95% of indian forest firest are human-caused and most of that is livelihood not arson. Ignition happens every year regardless, what changes the course of events is the fuel connectivity and health of the forest. For example, in Maharastra, Farmers for Forests works with tribal communities that light good firest to harvest the Mahua flowers. Such a litter fire under Mahua in a closed canopy and no invasive is just a litter fire. The same ignition in an invasive choked landscape is a wildfire. A similar story relates Tendu and Beedi leaves in Telengana, and yet again in the Amazon for cattle grazing. 

A fire ban backfires. The Soliga of BRT are forbidden controlled litter burns. Lantana covered 4.3% of BRT in 1997. Now it covers more than 50% of the reserve.

However the meta questions is can healthy forests support alternate livelihood options that don't result in uncontrolled destruction? and simultaneously, can we work on controlling the destruction on our way there, while enabling communities to still make their livelihoods. 

NTFPs and agroforestry are the same mechanics under different names. NTFPs pay in mature forests where honey, resin and other utilitie can be collected in modest amounts, but forever, as a supplemental income. This requires the forest to be healthy and resilient. Agroforestry on the other hand, works on the edges and often under the shade of a canopy. Most importantly, it does not carry fire the way invasives do. The edge plots of agroforestry, when done right, can turn into natural fire breaks. 

Aadhimalai Pazhangudiyinar Producer Company founded in 2013 with Keystone Foundation's support embodies both these principles. It is a collective of tribal producers; farmers, foragers and cattle rearers that work to supply (is last forest a multinational?) multinationsl like last forest with NTFP and agroforestry produce.
